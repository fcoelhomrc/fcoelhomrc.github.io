DeepSeek V4 Flash agreed with my hand labels at Cohen's kappa 0.83 on 81 statements. All seven of its errors accepted a claim the context did not support. Its faithfulness scores are therefore an upper bound.

The data is from [papers-please](https://github.com/fcoelhomrc/papers-please) at commit `361ff06` (12 Sep 2026).

## The judge answers one yes-or-no question

Ragas faithfulness splits an answer into statements, then asks the judge whether the context supports each one. I tested only the second step, because the split adds variance unrelated to the judge. The judge sees a passage and a statement and answers 1 or 0.

<Diagram src="blog/rag-eval/judge.svg" alt="83 statements go two ways. I label each one without seeing my intended answer, keeping 81 labels after dropping 2 as unsure. DeepSeek V4 Flash answers 1 or 0 for each. Comparing the two gives kappa 0.83 and accuracy 0.91." />

## Kappa corrects for chance agreement

43% of the statements are supported, so a judge that always answers 0 scores 57% accuracy. Cohen's kappa removes the agreement expected by chance: 0 is chance and 1 is perfect.

Across 21 judges on MT-Bench, exact-match agreement overstated kappa by 33.8 to 41.3 points. There, 85% agreement meant a kappa of about 0.48 ([Norman, Rivera and Hughes, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544v1)). I set the pass mark at kappa 0.6 before seeing any data.

## Each statement targets one failure

I wrote 83 statements against real abstracts from the corpus.

| Case type | n | It catches a judge that |
|---|---:|---|
| Supported | 29 | is too strict |
| Paraphrase | 11 | wants the passage's exact words |
| True but absent | 8 | checks whether a claim is true instead of whether the passage states it |
| Wrong entity | 7 | misses a swapped subject |
| Wrong number | 6 | misses 94% turned into 84% |
| Overgeneralised | 6 | misses a dropped scope qualifier |
| Unsupported cause | 6 | accepts a "because" the passage never gives |
| Unrelated | 5 | accepts text about something else |
| Negation | 5 | misses a reversed claim |

I labelled all 83 on a page that hid my intended answer and the case type. I dropped 2 as unsure, which left 81.

My labels matched my construction on 77 of 81 (95.1%). All four mismatches were statements I had built as supported. Two of them drop the qualifier "on flat ground", which my own labelling rules count as unsupported, so the human labels stand.

## The judge's errors all go one way

On all 81 statements, kappa was 0.828 and accuracy 0.914. Without the four disputed statements, kappa was 0.922 and accuracy 0.961 on 77.

Three errors are true-but-absent statements: correct facts the passage does not state. The other four are the disputed statements, which I worked out from the two accuracy figures (77 statements at 0.961 leave three errors). The judge made no errors on wrong entities, wrong numbers, negations, unsupported causes or unrelated text.

A faithfulness score of 0.90 from this judge therefore likely overstates the true figure. I measured only the judge I had already chosen; a comparison of four candidate judges is designed in the notes and not built.

## Judged runs agree with the labels and share their bias

Qwen3 30B answered all 100 questions from the top 10 chunks of each retriever, and the judge scored four Ragas metrics ([#48](https://github.com/fcoelhomrc/papers-please/issues/48)). BM25 led on faithfulness (0.896), context recall (0.841) and context precision (0.693).

BM25 never abstained, and each encoder abstained three or four times. These runs use the same 100 questions as part 2, so they share its vocabulary bias toward BM25.

The judge rates BM25 retrieval higher than the labels do. Its context recall is 0.841 against a label recall of 0.797, and its context precision is 0.693 against a MAP of 0.626.

Two causes fit, and this data cannot separate them. The labels miss relevant chunks that were not seeds, and the judge is lenient.

Query rewrites look the same through the judge as in part 2. With Arctic, context recall falls from 0.832 for the original question to 0.70 for HyDE. Decomposition with the original added back is level, at 0.866.

## Cost

The four judged runs in #48 cost $0.765 together, about $0.19 per run of 100 questions. The project notes estimate Claude Haiku 4.5 at about 15 times that.

## Limits

- 83 statements give a direction, with wide error bars on kappa.
- One person labelled every statement, so there is no human-to-human agreement to compare with.
- I wrote the cases, so they only test failures I anticipated.
