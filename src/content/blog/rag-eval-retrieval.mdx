This guide scores retrievers using the 100 questions from [part 1](../rag-eval-test-set/), each tagged with the ids of the chunks that answer it. It explains the metrics with a worked example, adds confidence intervals, and then compares five retrievers, a reranker and four ways of rewriting the question.

The short version: plain keyword search (BM25) beat every embedding model on its own. Combining BM25 with the best embedding model beat BM25 alone. The reranker and the question rewrites never helped.

## The retrievers we compare

A retriever takes a question and returns a ranked list of chunks. We test these approaches:

| Method | How it ranks chunks |
|---|---|
| BM25 | Counts the question's words in each chunk, giving rare words more weight and adjusting for chunk length |
| Postgres `ts_rank` | Also counts matching words, but without the rare-word weighting or length adjustment |
| Dense | An embedding model turns question and chunk into vectors; chunks whose vectors are closest rank first |
| Hybrid | Runs a keyword ranker and a dense ranker, then merges the two lists |

We test four embedding models for the dense side: bge-small, bge-large, Arctic Embed M v2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B.

The hybrid merges lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF). Each chunk scores 1 / (60 + rank) in every list it appears in, and the scores add up. A chunk ranked high in both lists wins, even if neither ranker put it first.

## Step 1: Score one question

A metric compares the retriever's ranking with the gold chunk ids. Take a question with two gold chunks, A and B, and a retriever whose top 5 is C, A, D, E, F.

| Metric | Question it answers | This example |
|---|---|---|
| Recall@5 | What share of the gold chunks is in the top 5? | 1 of 2 = 0.5 |
| Precision@5 | What share of the top 5 is gold? | 1 of 5 = 0.2 |
| Hit rate | Is any gold chunk in the top 5? | Yes = 1 |
| MRR | One over the rank of the first gold chunk | 1/2 = 0.5 |
| nDCG@5 | How near the top are the gold chunks, compared with a perfect ranking? | 0.39 |

nDCG gives each gold chunk a credit of 1 / log₂(rank + 1), so rank 1 earns 1 and rank 2 earns 0.63. A perfect ranking here puts A and B first and earns 1.63. The example earns 0.63, and 0.63 / 1.63 = 0.39.

Every metric is a mean over all 100 questions. None of this calls a model, so a full sweep runs in minutes and costs nothing.

## Step 2: Add confidence intervals

A mean over 100 questions is noisy. The 100 scores you already have give the error bar directly: the standard error is their standard deviation divided by √100. No extra runs are needed.

To compare two setups, take the difference on each question and average those 100 differences. That gives a narrower interval than comparing two separate means, because hard questions are hard for both setups.

At this size the intervals are wide. A real 5-point gap in recall gets detected only about 18% of the time. Treat any gap under 5 points as a tie unless its interval excludes zero.

## Result 1: BM25 beats every embedding model on its own

On its own, every embedding model scored below BM25 (0.705 nDCG at 10). Arctic came closest at 0.664.

The model family mattered more than its size. Going from bge-small to bge-large gained 0.019 nDCG, while switching to Arctic gained 0.097.

Keyword search has an advantage here that real users may not give it. The test questions were written from the chunks, so they reuse the chunks' exact words.

Merging also needs balanced weights. An earlier version gave the keyword list a weight of 0.1, so its top hit scored 0.1 / 61 = 0.0016. That is below a dense chunk at rank 40 (0.0100), so keyword results could not rise until the weight became 1.0.

## Result 3: The reranker never helped

A reranker is a second, slower model. A cross-encoder reads the question and one chunk together and outputs a relevance score, so it is applied only to the top candidates.

We tested `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` on every encoder. For each one, the best setup without the reranker beat the best setup with it, by 0.048 to 0.094 nDCG.

The model was trained on web search queries, which look little like questions about research papers. I have not tested whether that explains it.

## Result 4: Rewriting the question made retrieval worse

Some pipelines rewrite the question before searching. We tested four rewrites, each generated once by an LLM and cached:

- Decomposition splits the question into sub-questions and searches each.
- Multi-query writes paraphrases and searches each.
- Both of those again, with the original question added back.
- HyDE writes a hypothetical answer and searches with that instead.

## What to use, and what to check next

The best setup measured is Arctic merged with BM25, with no reranker and no rewrite. It reaches nDCG 0.760 in the top 10 and 0.780 in the top 50. It takes about 2.1 seconds per query, against 0.7 seconds for dense search alone.

The open question is the test set itself. Its questions copy the papers' wording, which favours BM25. Questions written in users' own words would show whether BM25 still wins.
