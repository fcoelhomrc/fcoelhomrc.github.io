On this corpus BM25 beat every dense encoder used on its own. Only Arctic Embed M v2, fused with BM25, beat BM25 alone: +0.055 nDCG@10 (95% CI +0.012 to +0.098). The cross-encoder reranker and all four query rewrites never helped.

Every number here compares retrieved chunk ids with the gold ids from [part 1](../rag-eval-test-set/), so none of it needed a model call. The data is from [papers-please](https://github.com/fcoelhomrc/papers-please) at commit `361ff06` (12 Sep 2026).

## Two scoring branches

Comparing ids costs nothing, so it runs after every change. Grading an answer with an LLM judge costs money, so it runs on demand; part 3 covers it.

<Diagram src="blog/rag-eval/branches.svg" alt="A question goes to BM25 and to a dense encoder. RRF fuses the two rankings and an optional cross-encoder reranks them. The free branch compares the top-k ids with gold ids. The paid branch has Qwen3 30B answer from the top-k chunks and DeepSeek V4 Flash grade the answer." maxWidth="30rem" />

| Metric | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Recall@k | What share of the gold chunks is in the top k? |
| Precision@k | What share of the top k is gold? It cannot exceed min(gold, k)/k |
| Hit rate | Is any gold chunk in the top k? |
| MRR | How high is the first gold chunk? |
| nDCG@k | How near the top are the gold chunks, with credit falling by rank? |
| MAP@k | What is the mean precision at each gold chunk's rank? |

## Confidence intervals need no extra calls

Each metric is a mean of 100 per-question scores, so the standard error is s/√n from their spread. To compare two setups, I take the difference per question, which gives a tighter interval.

With 100 questions, a paired test detects a real 5-point recall gap only about 18% of the time. I treat gaps under 5 points as ties unless the paired interval excludes zero (`docs/rag-evaluation.md`, section 8).

## Fusing Arctic with BM25 gives the highest recall

Fused with BM25, Arctic has the highest recall at every depth. With the other three encoders, the fused line sits near BM25 alone.

Postgres `ts_rank` trails BM25 because its two-argument form has no inverse document frequency and no length normalisation.

Fusion first needed a weight fix. The keyword side had weight 0.1, fitted on an older 12-paper corpus. A rank-1 keyword hit then scored 0.0016, below the 0.0100 of a rank-40 dense hit ([#47](https://github.com/fcoelhomrc/papers-please/issues/47)).

A sweep put weight 1.0 first for every encoder, and all fused numbers here use it.

## The reranker never helped

For every encoder, the best setup without the cross-encoder beat the best setup with it, by 0.048 to 0.094 nDCG ([#49](https://github.com/fcoelhomrc/papers-please/issues/49)).

At matched output size, the reranker cost Arctic 0.05 to 0.10 nDCG. The bge models lost 0.02 to 0.04, inside the intervals, and Qwen3 tied at k=1.

I have not tested why. The model, `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`, was trained on web search queries, and paper text may be too far from them.

## The original question beat every rewrite

I tested four rewrites: sub-questions, paraphrases, each of those with the original added back, and HyDE, which searches with a generated answer. The original question ranked first at every depth, for every encoder and for BM25.

Paraphrases lost the most, and HyDE lost more still with Arctic. My guess is that these questions already use the paper's words, so any rewrite moves away from them.

## What I would use

The best measured setup is Arctic fused with BM25, with no reranker and no rewrite: nDCG 0.760 at k=10 and 0.780 at k=50. It took about 2.1 seconds per query, against 0.7 for dense alone. The app's `config.yaml` at `361ff06` still uses bge-large.

The test questions reuse the papers' words, which favours BM25. Whether BM25 still wins on questions in a user's own words is untested.
